The concept

A real trail-running camp — with enough space to recover.

Four purposeful days in the mountains. Your own room when you need quiet. A group dinner when you want company.

Two athletes, one small group.

Monika has raced Worlds for Lithuania and lives on these trails. Anna is Icelandic national team with a Laugavegur podium and sub-3 marathon speed. Both bring their experience and a love of running in the mountains.

Experience shared on the trail.

We'll run alongside you, share what we've learned from racing and training on technical terrain, and help you become more comfortable in the mountains.

Why only eight?

Because eight keeps the group small enough for two hosts to run alongside everyone. You'll never be a bib number here. You'll be the one whose descent line gets shouted compliments.

Day three

The Long Day

This edition: Serra de Bèrnia. About 22 km, with 1,400 m of climbing.

No heroic summit, because there isn't one here. The Marina Alta works differently: it accumulates. Everything from days one and two (lines, feet, poles, fuelling, pacing) at effort, for hours.

Twenty minutes of silence

Sounds woo-woo. Isn't. The last two hours of your ultra will be exactly this quiet, and it's better to meet that silence before race day.

The last runner has company

On any climb, always. Two of us, eight of you. The ratio is the point.

Then the sea

Ten minutes away, seventeen degrees. You'll swear a little. Everyone talks about it at dinner. Non-negotiable.

Every edition's Long Day is a different line. Bèrnia this time, Benigembla another year, some editions a race bib. Each one goes in the camp logbook, with the weather and all eight names. You take the day's profile home on a locally made tile. Earned on the day. Not for sale.

Questions worth answering early

The stuff you'd ask us over coffee, answered before you apply.

I'm scared I'll be the slowest.+

Someone will be. She gets one of us beside her on every climb and the same paella at dinner. This camp doesn't do dropped runners.

What can four days actually change?+

Four days give you time to explore technical terrain with company, try different approaches and ask questions as they come up. We'll share what we've learned and offer feedback, with ideas to take home. We are experienced athletes, not certified coaches, and the camp does not include an individual training plan. Fitness takes longer than a weekend.

How fit do I need to be?+

Comfortable running 15 km on rolling ground and happy to hike the steep stuff. The Long Day adapts to the group. This is a real training camp, but 'real' measures seriousness, not speed.

Do I need trail experience?+

Some helps. If you've got road fitness and rocky ground makes you nervous, you're exactly who this is for.

Is it women only?+

Yes. This edition is women-only, hosts included.

What trails will we run?+

Routes are selected from the Denia, Javea, Bernia, Vall de Laguar, and Marina Alta area depending on weather, group level, and permissions.

What if the weather turns?+

There will be lower-risk backup routes, road-to-trail options, workshop swaps, and heat or storm protocols. Mountain plans need escape routes.

Can I come alone?+

Yes, almost everyone does. Everyone has her own private room, with plenty of time together on the trails and over dinner. By day two the strangers thing is over.

My flight lands at 11pm. Is that a problem?+

No. Wednesday is arrival day: no training, no schedule, a pickup whenever you land, and dinner kept warm if it's late. The running starts Thursday morning, once everyone has slept. Monday works the same in reverse, so book whichever flight home is cheapest.

What kit is mandatory?+

Trail shoes, hydration vest, wind shell, sun protection, emergency blanket, phone, personal meds. The full list arrives before camp, with our honest shoe opinions in it.

Steal our homework: five Marina Alta routes, free. GPX files, water points, which lines actually go, and where people get cliffed.
